# Sponsors and Chicanes

Local businesses are not just donors. At a Rabenregatta, **every sponsor becomes part of the race**. They pay for a prize, build a themed obstacle for the course, and decide what their prize is for.

The goal is **identification, not advertising**. People should say: *"That's our dog school's leash curtain!"* and not *"There's a logo."*

## The sponsor deal

Each sponsor:

| Gives | Gets |
|---|---|
| **A prize**: money for prize bags, vouchers or goods | Their name on the prize, e.g. *"Underdog Prize by the dog school"* |
| **A chicane**: an obstacle on the course that fits their business | A sign at their chicane, mentions by the commentator, photos of "their" chicane |
| **A win condition**: they decide what their prize is for | A moment on stage at the award ceremony |
| Optional: **a workshop** with their staff | A team event and a real connection to the village |

Use the [sponsor sheet](../templates/sponsor-sheet.md) to agree the details.

## Example: the dog school

| | |
|---|---|
| **Prize** | A book voucher worth €20 in every prize bag |
| **Win condition** | **Underdog Prize**: the slowest boat that still reaches the finish |
| **Chicane** | Dog leashes hanging from a line across the creek into the water. Boats must find their way through the "leash curtain" |

Why it works: it's funny, it fits the business, and the Underdog Prize rewards boats that would never win a speed race. That's exactly the variety of prizes we want. See [Race format: prizes](08-Race-Format.md#prizes).

## Win conditions

The sponsor chooses, the organisers check. A good win condition is:

- **Measurable.** Everyone can see who won. Weigh, measure, time or count, and don't argue.
- **Fair for all ages.** Or awarded per age group.
- **Announced early.** All prizes and win conditions are on **every announcement, months before the event**. Builders can then design for a prize they like. A child who knows there's a prize for the heaviest boat builds very differently. More different goals mean more children with a real chance.
- **Tied to the race.** By default, a boat must **finish its race** (not disqualified) to win. The heaviest boat that sinks at the start doesn't count. That makes every prize a real engineering challenge.

For a list of tested ideas, see the [prize catalogue](08-Race-Format.md#prize-catalogue).

## Chicanes

A chicane is an obstacle on the course. It makes the race exciting and gives each sponsor a visible place on the creek.

### Rules for every chicane

1. **Passable.** At least one boat at a time must be able to get through. Test it at the test run.
2. **Safe for people.** No sharp edges, no tripping hazards on the banks, nothing that could fall on anyone.
3. **Safe for the creek.** Nothing may come loose, dissolve or rub off in the water. No oil, no fresh paint, no loose small parts.
4. **Doesn't block the creek.** Water, fish and small animals must still pass freely.
5. **Firmly fixed.** It must not float away, even if the water rises.
6. **Removed the same day.** Nothing stays in the creek overnight. Animals can get tangled in lines, nets and leashes.
7. **Checked.** The creek and safety team approves every chicane before the race.

### Ideas by business

Be creative! The best chicanes tell a little story about the business.

| Business | Chicane idea |
|---|---|
| Dog school | Leash curtain: leashes hanging into the water |
| Carpenter, joiner | Wooden slalom gates, a narrow "harbour entrance" |
| Sawmill | A floating log boom with a gap |
| Chimney sweep | Clinker bricks or granite blocks placed in the creek: a rocky obstacle course |
| Bakery | A concrete pretzel or bread loaf on the creek bed (see workshop below) |
| Plumber | A pipe tunnel: a short, wide, clean pipe the boats float through |
| Fire brigade | A gentle spray tunnel from the bank |
| Bank, savings bank | A giant concrete coin in the creek, with a gap to slip past |
| Garden centre | A "reed island" of potted native plants on the bank edge |
| Farm | A gate from a real old fence post and a board |
| Hairdresser | A "brush" of natural fibre string hanging into the water |
| Village shop | A concrete shopping basket as an island |

**Best materials:** natural stone (granite, sandstone), fired clay bricks and clinker, untreated wood. They are inert, so they don't release anything into the water, and they can go in the creek right away.

**Please avoid** old car tyres, rubber mats and anything that releases microplastics or other harmful substances. Also avoid loose straw or hay, which washes away.

## Sponsor workshops: make your own chicane

This is a great way to create identification. The sponsor's team makes their own chicane together. Casting in concrete is **one example**. Be creative: the team can also carve wood, paint stones or build a gate.

### Example: cast your product in concrete

**The idea:** The staff cast a symbol of their business in concrete, for example a pretzel, a coin, a paw, a tooth or a house. Then they paint it and bring it to the race as their chicane. Afterwards, it can go into their shop window.

**How it works:**

1. **4–6 weeks before the race:** Workshop with the sponsor's staff (2–3 hours). Build a simple mould from a bucket, a box, a sand bed or a silicone form. Mix the concrete and cast it.
2. **Let it cure for at least 4 weeks.** Fresh concrete makes water alkaline. Cured concrete is used in rivers everywhere, but it needs time.
3. **Rinse it well** several times before it goes into the creek.
4. **Paint only above the waterline**, or place the chicane at the bank so the painted part stays dry. Use water-based outdoor paint and let it dry completely. Paint under water flakes off over time.
5. **Weight:** A concrete chicane is heavy. Adults carry and place it, with gloves. Make sure it sits firmly on the creek bed and can't tip over.
6. **Remove it after the race.** It goes back to the sponsor.

**Invite a builder crew:** Children can join the sponsor's workshop as "junior designers". This connects generations and businesses.

**Other workshop ideas:**

- Carpenter's team builds slalom gates with a group of children
- Garden centre plants a native "reed island" for the bank with children
- Bakery bakes raven cookies for the award ceremony
- Sponsor staff build their own boats for the grown-up ravens class

## Recognition without a sponsor show

- A **small wooden sign** at each chicane: *"Leash curtain, by [dog school]"*
- The commentator introduces each chicane before the first heat
- Prize named after the sponsor; the sponsor hands it over
- Photos of the chicane and the boats passing it go to the sponsor afterwards
- Sponsors on the poster, in one line, same size for all

Keep the focus on the builders. The sponsors are part of the story, not the headline.
