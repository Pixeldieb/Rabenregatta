# Money and Fairness

**Profit is not the goal. Community is.** Rabenregatta should cost as little as possible, and nobody should stay at home because of money.

## The ground rules

1. **Free for everyone aged 0–18. This is a must.** Children and teenagers never pay a start fee, a workshop fee or a material fee.
2. **Materials cost nothing.** They are donated, collected as litter or found in nature. See [Materials traffic light](05-Materials-Traffic-Light.md#where-to-find-materials).
3. **Every child gets an ice cream voucher.**
4. **Grown-ups pay a small start fee** in their own class.
5. **Money that comes in goes back into the event**: prizes, ice cream, equipment for next year.

## Ice cream vouchers

Every participating child gets a voucher for one ice cream. You can decide to give one to every child present, if your budget allows.

**How it works:**

1. **Before the event:** Agree with a local ice cream vendor (ice cream parlour, café, ice cream van) on a **fixed price per voucher**.
2. **Make the vouchers:** numbered and stamped, so they can't simply be copied. See [ice cream voucher template](../templates/ice-cream-voucher.md).
3. **At registration:** hand out one voucher per child and note the number range handed out.
4. **During the event:** children redeem them with the vendor.
5. **Afterwards:** the vendor hands in the collected vouchers. You count them together, and the organiser pays according to the number of vouchers (against an invoice).

This way you only pay for ice cream that was actually eaten, and the vendor has a good day too. Ideally the ice cream vendor is on site, or within walking distance.

## Grown-up start fee

Adults race in their own class, the **Grown-up ravens (18+)**, and pay a **small start fee**, for example €3–5. It covers part of the ice cream vouchers and prizes.

- Family teams with at least one child are **free**. The boat counts as the child's boat.
- Adult helpers, jury members and people accompanying children pay nothing.
- Say openly what the money is used for. *"Your start fee pays for the children's ice cream."* Most adults are happy to pay.
- Keep a simple list of income with a cash box and a receipt book. If a club organises the event, follow its bookkeeping rules.

## Duck race lottery (optional)

Want to raise donations? Add a **rubber duck race** as the grand finale after the boat final.

**How it works:**

1. People "adopt" a numbered rubber duck for a small donation (e.g. €2).
2. All ducks are released at the start at the same time.
3. The first ducks at the finish win small prizes, ideally donated by sponsors.

**Rules for the ducks:**

- Buy the ducks **once** and reuse them every year. Number them with a waterproof marker or a burned-in number.
- Only use **intact, sealed** ducks. Ducks with a hole fill with water and sink.
- **Count them out and count them back in.** The catch net catches them. Every duck comes back out of the creek.
- Rubber ducks are plastic. That's why they are only okay as a reused, fully recovered racing set.

> **Note:** Public lotteries and raffles may need a permit or notification, depending on your country and region. In Germany, ask your municipality's public order office (*Ordnungsamt*). This page is not legal advice.

## Budget reality check

A typical Rabenregatta pays for itself like this:

| Money in | Money out |
|---|---|
| Sponsor prizes (paid directly by the sponsor) | Ice cream vouchers |
| Grown-up start fees | Printing |
| Duck race donations | Consumables (string, sandpaper, glue) |
| Café or cake sale by a local club | First aid refill |
| Municipality or funding programme | Equipment (once) |

See the [budget template](../templates/budget.md).

**If money is left over:** Put it into the equipment kit, start the ice cream fund for next year, or donate it to a local children's project. Tell people what you did with it.
