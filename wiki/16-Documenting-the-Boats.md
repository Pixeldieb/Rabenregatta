# Documenting the Boats

**Take pictures of every boat!** A boat lasts one summer. A photo lasts forever.

## Why it matters

- **Pride:** A photo of your own boat is proof: *"I built that."* It keeps the self-efficacy experience alive long after race day ([Bandura, 1997](Sources.md#bandura-1997)).
- **Inspiration:** Next year's builders see what is possible. This is what Bandura calls *vicarious experience*: "Someone like me did that." Photos inspire; they are not blueprints. Last year's boats can't race again.
- **Prizes:** Weight, height and materials are recorded at registration. The photo shows the boat as it was registered, and it stays that way.
- **Sponsors:** They love photos of boats passing *their* chicane.
- **The press:** A good boat photo is often the best picture for the local paper.
- **This kit:** Photos help other villages. See [CONTRIBUTING](../CONTRIBUTING.md).

## The photo station

Set up a small **photo station** right next to the registration. Every boat stops there before it goes into the gallery.

**You need:**

- A table with a **neutral background**: a light sheet of fabric or a large board
- An **A4 grid mat**: an A4 frame drawn on the table cover, with a 1 cm grid. It shows the size at a glance
- The boat's **start number** (cloakroom ticket) visible in each photo
- A camera or phone, ideally on a small tripod, always at the same distance
- Good light: outdoors in the shade, or a white umbrella against direct sun
- One **photographer** responsible for the station

**Take three photos per boat:**

1. **Side view** at water level (table level)
2. **Top view** on the A4 grid mat
3. **Detail**: the cleverest part, chosen by the builder (*"What should we photograph?"* is a great question!)

Optional: a fourth photo of **builder and boat together**, only with photo consent, and only for the family, not for publication.

## Record the boat data

At the same time, the race office fills in the [boat register](../templates/boat-register.md):

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Start number, **boat name** (never the builder's name) | Basic data |
| **Weight** (kitchen scale, grams) | Prizes, boat statistics |
| **Height** (ruler, cm) | Prizes, boat statistics. There is no height limit, which makes the data interesting |
| Race time, disqualification, prizes | Filled in during and after the race |
| Main materials, photo numbers (optional) | Statistics, matching photos and boats |

## During the race

- One person photographs **each chicane** as the boats pass. These are the most exciting pictures.
- Short slow-motion video at the **finish line**. It also helps judges with close finishes.
- The **award ceremony**: winners with their boats (with consent).

## File names and archive

Name the files the same way every time:

```text
YEAR-village-boat-NUMBER-view.jpg
2027-ourvillage-boat-017-side.jpg
2027-ourvillage-boat-017-top.jpg
2027-ourvillage-chicane-dogschool-01.jpg
```

Store them in a shared folder straight after the event, together with the boat register. For this repository, choose the best ones (boats only), shrink them and add them to `assets/images/events/YEAR-village/`.

## Privacy

- **Boats, hands and chicanes** can almost always be photographed without problems.
- **Faces** only with consent. See [photo consent](../templates/photo-consent.md).
- People who don't want to be photographed get a coloured sticker or wristband. The photographer knows the colour.
- Published photos and lists show **boat names only**, never the names of builders.

> **Note:** Photo and privacy rules differ by country (e.g. GDPR in the EU). This page is not legal advice.

## Online gallery and fan culture

Put every boat into an **online gallery**: one card per boat, year after year. Over time, this becomes the **hall of fame** of your Rabenregatta. Fans start to have favourite boats, builders come back to beat their own records, and the whole village looks forward to next year.

**One card per boat:**

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Boat name | Cork Express |
| Year, start number | 2027, no. 17 |
| Photos | side, top, detail |
| Weight | 412 g |
| Height | 18 cm |
| Race time | 1:47 (or DSQ) |
| Prizes | 🐢 Underdog, ❤️ Audience |
| Main materials | 23 wine corks, skewers, cotton sail |
| Boat type | raft / monohull / catamaran / trimaran / other |

**Only boat data, never the builder's name.** Families who want to can say "that's ours" themselves.

**Why the data matters:** With weight, height, type and time for every boat, you can show interesting things after a few years: Are catamarans faster than rafts? How heavy was the heaviest boat that still finished? From what height do boats capsize? That's real science with village data, and it's great material for school lessons.

**Ideas for more fan culture:**

- **Boat trading cards:** Print a card per boat with photo and data, like a top trumps game. Compare weight, height, time. Children love to collect and swap them.
- **Records board:** fastest time ever, heaviest boat ever, tallest boat that finished.
- **"Boat of the year"** poster in the town hall or the school.
- **Return of the builders:** "The builder of last year's Underdog is back with a new boat!"

**Where to host it:** A simple web page, a folder in this repository (see [gallery template](../gallery/README.md)), or a photo album on the organiser's website. Only publish boat photos without people, unless you have consent.

## Give something back

- Send each family the photos of their boat (e.g. by e-mail link, only their own boat).
- Print a photo on the **certificate**, if you can print on site or send the certificates later.
- Make a small **boat gallery** for the village: in the town hall, the school or online.
