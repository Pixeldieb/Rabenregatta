# Why Rabenregatta?

A small boat on a small creek, and yet something big can happen. This page explains the educational idea and the research behind it.

## The goal: self-efficacy

**Self-efficacy** means believing: *"I can handle this. My actions make a difference."*

The psychologist Albert Bandura showed that this belief strongly affects what people try, how hard they work and how long they keep going when things get difficult ([Bandura, 1977](Sources.md#bandura-1977); [Bandura, 1997](Sources.md#bandura-1997)).

Self-efficacy is not a fixed trait. It can grow. Bandura named **four sources** that make it grow. Rabenregatta is built around all four.

| Source (Bandura) | What it means | How Rabenregatta uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Mastery experience** (strongest source) | "I did it myself, and it worked." | Every participant designs, builds and floats their **own** boat. No kits, no parents taking over. |
| **2. Vicarious experience** | "Someone like me did it, so I can too." | Children see other children and neighbours build. Mixed age groups and family teams. |
| **3. Verbal persuasion** | Honest encouragement from others | Helpers ask questions instead of giving answers. Prizes for creativity, reuse and seaworthiness, not just speed. |
| **4. Physiological and emotional state** | Feeling calm and safe, not anxious | Low stakes, a playful mood, a village party atmosphere. Sinking is allowed and expected. |

## More ideas that support the design

### Learning by making (constructionism)

People learn especially well when they build something real that they can show to others ([Papert, 1980](Sources.md#papert-1980)). A boat that has to float is honest feedback: physics does not lie. Maker education builds on this idea ([Martinez & Stager, 2013](Sources.md#martinez-stager-2013)).

### Learning from experience

Kolb describes learning as a cycle: **do → notice → think → try again** ([Kolb, 1984](Sources.md#kolb-1984)). The test tank at the workshop and the heats on race day give participants several turns through this cycle.

### Failing is useful

Research on "productive failure" shows that people who first struggle with a problem often understand it more deeply later ([Kapur, 2008](Sources.md#kapur-2008)). A boat that tips over is a lesson, not a defeat. This also fits the idea of a **growth mindset**: abilities grow through effort ([Dweck, 2006](Sources.md#dweck-2006)).

### Motivation from within

People are most motivated when they experience **autonomy** (my choice), **competence** (I can do it) and **relatedness** (I belong) ([Ryan & Deci, 2000](Sources.md#ryan-deci-2000)). Rabenregatta offers free design (autonomy), a real test (competence) and a village event (relatedness).

### We love what we make

People value things more when they built them themselves. Researchers call this the "IKEA effect" ([Norton, Mochon & Ariely, 2012](Sources.md#norton-2012)). A self-built boat is a treasure. That is also why the "no copying" rule matters.

### Learning in your own place

Place-based education uses the local landscape and community as the classroom ([Sobel, 2004](Sources.md#sobel-2004)). Children who spend time in nature benefit in many ways, from well-being to learning ([Chawla, 2015](Sources.md#chawla-2015)). The village creek becomes a place of discovery.

### Stronger villages

Shared activities build trust and networks between neighbours. Putnam calls this **social capital** ([Putnam, 2000](Sources.md#putnam-2000)). The carpenter donates offcuts, the fire brigade secures the creek, the bakery sells cake, and people meet who otherwise would not.

### Sustainability in practice

The UNESCO programme "ESD for 2030" calls for education that enables people to act for a sustainable future ([UNESCO, 2020](Sources.md#unesco-2020)). Rabenregatta makes this concrete: participants collect waste, give it a new use, and take everything back out of the water. This follows the EU waste hierarchy: **prevent → reuse → recycle** ([Directive 2008/98/EC](Sources.md#eu-waste-2008)).

## Be honest about the evidence

The research above explains **why** we designed Rabenregatta this way. To our knowledge, no study has yet measured the effects of Rabenregatta itself. So treat the benefits as well-founded **assumptions**, not proven results.

You can help change that: [Evaluation](12-Evaluation.md) shows a simple way to measure your event.

## Design principles, summarised

1. **Your boat, your idea.** Adults help with safety and questions, never with building.
2. **A clear, tiny frame.** A4 limits size and cost and makes everyone equal. Limits spark creativity.
3. **Many ways to win.** Speed, creativity, reuse, seaworthiness.
4. **Failure is welcome.** Test, sink, improve.
5. **The village is the classroom.** Local creek, local materials, local people.
6. **Leave no trace.** Nature is a partner, not a backdrop.
