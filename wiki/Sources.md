# Sources

These are the studies and books behind the Rabenregatta concept. Each entry has a one-line summary in plain language. Please read the originals before you quote them in funding applications or publications.

Found a good source? Add it. See [CONTRIBUTING](../CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Self-efficacy and motivation

<a id="bandura-1977"></a>
**Bandura, A. (1977).** Self-efficacy: Toward a unifying theory of behavioral change. *Psychological Review, 84*(2), 191–215. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.84.2.191>
→ The founding paper: belief in your own abilities shapes behaviour. It names four sources of self-efficacy, with mastery experiences as the strongest.

<a id="bandura-1997"></a>
**Bandura, A. (1997).** *Self-efficacy: The exercise of control.* W. H. Freeman.
→ The comprehensive book on self-efficacy, including its role in education and communities.

<a id="schwarzer-1995"></a>
**Schwarzer, R., & Jerusalem, M. (1995).** Generalized Self-Efficacy scale. In J. Weinman, S. Wright, & M. Johnston (Eds.), *Measures in health psychology: A user's portfolio. Causal and control beliefs* (pp. 35–37). NFER-NELSON.
→ A short, validated questionnaire (10 items) to measure general self-efficacy. Available in many languages.

<a id="ryan-deci-2000"></a>
**Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2000).** Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being. *American Psychologist, 55*(1), 68–78. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68>
→ People are motivated from within when they experience autonomy, competence and relatedness.

<a id="dweck-2006"></a>
**Dweck, C. S. (2006).** *Mindset: The new psychology of success.* Random House.
→ People who believe abilities can grow ("growth mindset") handle setbacks better.

<a id="csikszentmihalyi-1990"></a>
**Csikszentmihalyi, M. (1990).** *Flow: The psychology of optimal experience.* Harper & Row.
→ Deep, joyful concentration happens when a task is challenging but manageable. That is exactly the A4 challenge.

## Learning by doing and making

<a id="papert-1980"></a>
**Papert, S. (1980).** *Mindstorms: Children, computers, and powerful ideas.* Basic Books.
→ The basis of constructionism: children learn best by building things they care about.

<a id="kolb-1984"></a>
**Kolb, D. A. (1984).** *Experiential learning: Experience as the source of learning and development.* Prentice Hall.
→ Learning happens in a cycle of experience, reflection, thinking and trying again.

<a id="kapur-2008"></a>
**Kapur, M. (2008).** Productive failure. *Cognition and Instruction, 26*(3), 379–424. <https://doi.org/10.1080/07370000802212669>
→ Struggling with a problem first, even failing, can lead to deeper understanding later.

<a id="martinez-stager-2013"></a>
**Martinez, S. L., & Stager, G. (2013).** *Invent to learn: Making, tinkering, and engineering in the classroom.* Constructing Modern Knowledge Press.
→ A practical guide to maker education, based on constructionism.

<a id="lave-wenger-1991"></a>
**Lave, J., & Wenger, E. (1991).** *Situated learning: Legitimate peripheral participation.* Cambridge University Press.
→ People learn by taking part in real activities of a community. Newcomers learn alongside the experienced.

<a id="norton-2012"></a>
**Norton, M. I., Mochon, D., & Ariely, D. (2012).** The IKEA effect: When labor leads to love. *Journal of Consumer Psychology, 22*(3), 453–460. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2011.08.002>
→ People value things more when they built them themselves, especially when they finished them successfully.

## Place, nature and community

<a id="sobel-2004"></a>
**Sobel, D. (2004).** *Place-based education: Connecting classrooms & communities.* The Orion Society.
→ Using the local environment and community as a place of learning strengthens learning and local involvement.

<a id="chawla-2015"></a>
**Chawla, L. (2015).** Benefits of nature contact for children. *Journal of Planning Literature, 30*(4), 433–452. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0885412215595441>
→ A review: contact with nature supports children's health, well-being, learning and pro-environmental attitudes.

<a id="putnam-2000"></a>
**Putnam, R. D. (2000).** *Bowling alone: The collapse and revival of American community.* Simon & Schuster.
→ Shared local activities build social capital: trust and networks that make communities stronger.

## Sustainability and environment

<a id="unesco-2020"></a>
**UNESCO (2020).** *Education for sustainable development: A roadmap (ESD for 2030).* UNESCO. <https://doi.org/10.54675/YFRE1448>
→ The global framework for education that empowers people to act for sustainability.

<a id="eu-waste-2008"></a>
**European Parliament & Council (2008).** Directive 2008/98/EC on waste (Waste Framework Directive), Article 4: waste hierarchy. <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2008/98/oj>
→ The priority order in the EU: prevention, preparing for reuse, recycling, other recovery, disposal.

<a id="gesamp-2015"></a>
**GESAMP (2015).** *Sources, fate and effects of microplastics in the marine environment: A global assessment* (Reports and Studies No. 90). IMO/FAO/UNESCO-IOC/UNIDO/WMO/IAEA/UN/UNEP/UNDP.
→ A UN expert report on how microplastics form, spread and harm living things.

## Physics of floating

<a id="openstax-physics"></a>
**Ling, S. J., Sanny, J., & Moebs, W. (2016).** *University Physics Volume 1*, Chapter 14: Fluid Mechanics. OpenStax. <https://openstax.org/details/books/university-physics-volume-1>
→ A free, open textbook that explains Archimedes' principle, density and buoyancy.
