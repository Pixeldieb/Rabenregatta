# Registration and Race Office

The race office is the heart of race day. It decides which boats race and in which order, and it records everything that makes a boat special. It also has a big influence on **how the event feels**. A calm, friendly registration where every boat is taken seriously is the first moment of pride for every builder.

## The basic rules

1. **Only registered boats race.** The race office calls boats to the start. A boat that is not registered is not called.
2. **First come, first served.** Boats race in the **order of registration**.
3. **Two lists.** Children and teens (0–18) and adults (over 18) are registered in **separate lists**. Adults don't get ice cream and pay a start fee, so they have their own process. See [Two lists](#two-lists-kids-and-grown-ups).
4. **As many boats as you like.** Everyone may register as many boats as they want. But each child gets **only one ice cream voucher**.
5. **Built today or brought along.** Boats can be built on site at the building stations, or built at home beforehand and brought along.
6. **Once registered, a boat is final.** After registration, a boat may **not be changed** any more. Say this clearly to every builder before you register the boat: *"Is your boat finished? After this, it stays as it is."*
7. **Every boat carries its number,** clearly visible.
8. **Registered boats go into the gallery** and wait there until their race.

## The boat, not the builder

**We register the name of the boat, not the name of the builder.**

The commentator announces *"Cork Express"*, not *"Mia"*. The results list shows boats, not children. This matters for the builders' feelings:

- When a boat sinks, **the boat** sank. The child did not fail in front of the whole village.
- When a boat wins, the builder is proud of *their* boat. That's all the self-efficacy needs: *"I built that."*
- A safe, relaxed mood is one of the four sources of self-efficacy ([Bandura, 1997](Sources.md#bandura-1997)). Fear of being embarrassed in public is its opposite.
- It also protects privacy. There are no children's names on lists, photos or in the online gallery.

Builders can tell people it's their boat whenever they want. It's their choice.

## What the race office records

A simple paper table, one for each list. See [boat register](../templates/boat-register.md).

| Field | How |
|---|---|
| **Boat name** | Given by the builder. No names of people |
| **Start number** | A numbered **cloakroom ticket** (double ticket) |
| **A4 check** | Does the boat fit on the A4 gauge? ✔ |
| **Weight** | Kitchen scale, in grams |
| **Height** | Ruler, from the lowest to the highest point, in cm |
| **Race time** | Filled in after the race, mm:ss |
| **Disqualified** | Filled in after the race, if needed |
| **Prizes** | One column per prize, to tick off and for documentation |

### Clusters of three

The paper table groups the boats in **clusters of three**: rows 1–3, 4–6, 7–9 and so on. Each cluster is **one heat**. Because boats are registered in order, the table is automatically the race order. The race office simply calls one cluster after the other.

## Two lists: kids and grown-ups

| | Kids and teens (0–18) | Grown-ups (over 18) |
|---|---|---|
| **List** | Kids' boat register | Grown-ups' boat register |
| **Fee** | Free | Small start fee, paid at registration |
| **Cloakroom tickets** | Roll in colour A, e.g. numbers 1–499 | Roll in colour B, e.g. numbers from 500. **Stub removed** before handing over |
| **Ice cream** | One voucher per child (the stub) | No |
| **Gold taler** | Yes | Yes |
| **Race order** | Clusters of three in registration order | Own block of clusters, by default **after** the kids' races |
| **Prizes** | All prizes | Decide per prize whether grown-ups can win it, or give them their own prizes. Announce it with the prize catalogue |

If possible, use a separate table or at least a separate person for the grown-ups' registration. The kids' registration must stay quick and friendly.

## Cloakroom tickets: start number and ice cream in one

Use **numbered cloakroom tickets** (double tickets from a roll, available in stationery shops):

- **One half goes on the boat** as the start number. Fix it firmly and visibly: tie it on, clip it with a wooden peg, or put it on a stick like a flag.
- **The tear-off half is the ice cream voucher.** The builder takes it to the ice cream vendor.
- The vendor collects the stubs. After the event, the organiser pays the vendor for the number of stubs. See [Money and fairness](15-Money-and-Fairness.md#ice-cream-vouchers).

### Only one ice cream per child

Children may register as many boats as they like, but each child gets **one ice cream voucher only**.

- For a child's first boat, the child gets the stub.
- For every further boat, the race office **tears off the stub first** and keeps it (mark it with a cross and put it in an envelope, so it can't be redeemed).
- Simply ask: *"Is this your first boat today?"* Trust the children.
- Optional: a small **ice cream stamp** on the back of the hand when a child gets its voucher. Then everyone can see it at a glance.
- Children without their own boat (for example siblings who built a boat together) get one voucher from a separate roll, also only one per child.
- Grown-ups' tickets come without a stub. See [Two lists](#two-lists-kids-and-grown-ups).

## The gallery

After registration, every boat goes into the **gallery**: tables or a line of boards near the start, with each boat's number clearly visible.

- The boats wait there until their cluster is called.
- **The audience vote takes place in the gallery.** Next to each boat is a voting container with the same number. People put their talers into the container of their favourite boat. See [Race format](08-Race-Format.md#the-audience-vote-gold-and-silver-talers).
- After its race, a boat goes back to its place in the gallery until the award ceremony.
- The gallery is a great place for spectators, the photo station, the commentator and the press.
- A helper keeps an eye on it: **no one touches the boats**, not even the builders. That's part of "once registered, a boat is final".

## Set-up

| Station | What you need |
|---|---|
| Registration table | Boat register, pens, cloakroom tickets, clipboard, cash box (grown-up start fees) |
| Check | A4 gauge, kitchen scale, ruler |
| Photo station | Right next to it. See [Documenting the boats](16-Documenting-the-Boats.md) |
| Gallery | Tables or boards, voting containers with numbers, talers |

Two to three people run registration: one talks to the builder and writes, one checks, weighs and measures, one takes the photos.

## Race office during the race

- Call the next cluster early: *"Next up: numbers 7, 8 and 9. Please bring your boats from the gallery to the start!"*
- Write down each boat's time and any disqualification straight away.
- Evaluate the prizes after the last race: fastest, slowest, heaviest, tallest, audience vote and every sponsor prize. Tick them off in the prize columns.
- The table is the **documentation** of the event. Keep it. It's the source for the results, the press, the certificates and the [online gallery](16-Documenting-the-Boats.md#online-gallery-and-fan-culture).
