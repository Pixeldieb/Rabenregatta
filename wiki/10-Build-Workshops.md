# Build Workshops

Workshops are where most of the learning happens. People can build at home, but open workshops make sure **everyone** can take part, even without tools or materials at home.

There are two kinds:

- **Building stations on race day:** In the morning of the race, the Euroboxes are open at the building stations. Many boats are built right there, then registered and put into the gallery.
- **Workshops before the event:** optional sessions in the weeks before, at school, in the village hall or with sponsors.

The tips on this page apply to both.

## Format

| | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Length** | 2–3 hours |
| **Group size** | 8–15 builders, 1 helper per 5 builders |
| **Number** | 2–4 sessions in the 6 weeks before the race |
| **Place** | School workshop, village hall, fire station, barn, outdoors in good weather |
| **Equipment** | The [Eurobox kit](06-Eurobox-System.md) plus a test harbour. Cover tables and benches with craft mats, especially borrowed ones |

## Sample session (2.5 hours)

| Time | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Arrive** | Name tags. Everyone picks up an A4 sheet: this is your boat's footprint |
| 0:10 | **Wonder** | Short experiment: what floats, what sinks? Test objects in the harbour. See [Why things float](../tutorials/01-why-things-float.md) |
| 0:25 | **Tool briefing** | Saw, cutter, pruners, hammer; the rules of the power tool station (5–10 min) |
| 0:35 | **Explore materials** | Look at the green and yellow boxes. Touch, weigh, test |
| 0:45 | **Sketch** | Draw your idea on the A4 sheet. No right or wrong |
| 1:00 | **Build** | Build the first version |
| 1:50 | **Test** | Into the test harbour! Does it float? Does it tip over? |
| 2:00 | **Improve** | Change one thing, test again |
| 2:20 | **Share** | Everyone shows their boat and says one thing they learned |
| 2:30 | **Tidy up** | Tools back to their boxes. Count them |

## The helper's role: ask, don't do

This is the most important rule for helpers. It is also the hardest one.

The self-efficacy effect only happens if the builder is really the one who solved the problem ([Bandura, 1997](Sources.md#bandura-1997)). If an adult takes over, even with good intentions, the child learns: *"I can't do this without help."*

**Instead of doing it, ask:**

| Instead of... | Try... |
|---|---|
| "Let me do that." | "What have you tried already?" |
| "It will tip over like that." | "What do you think will happen in the water?" |
| "Use two bottles here." | "What could make it more stable?" |
| "That's wrong." | "Interesting! Let's test it." |
| "Great job!" | "You figured out how to fix the mast. How did you do it?" |

**When helpers may step in:**

- Safety: tools used dangerously, sharp edges
- Physically impossible tasks: sawing thick wood for small children. Then: the child marks the line and holds, the adult saws, and the child decides everything else.
- Frustration: help with a question or a small hint, not a solution

## Test, sink, improve

The **test harbour** is the heart of the workshop. Celebrate every test, including the ones that sink. A good phrase for helpers: *"Now we know something new!"*

A **builder's logbook** helps older participants: write down what you changed and what happened. That is the engineering design process in action.

## No previous designs

Each boat must be a new design by its builder. That means:

- No kits, no downloaded plans, no boat from last year
- **Allowed:** learning from tutorials about principles (floating, stability, sails), looking at real boats, being inspired by other people's ideas and then building your own version

Why? The self-efficacy experience depends on "I found the solution" ([Bandura, 1977](Sources.md#bandura-1977)). And people value what they create themselves much more ([Norton et al., 2012](Sources.md#norton-2012)).

## Inclusion

- Offer workshops at different times (after school, Saturday).
- Offer tools for left-handers and easy-grip tools.
- Make sure girls and boys, young and old, all get to the tools. Mixed teams help.
- Picture cards for the tool briefing help children who cannot read yet and people who do not speak the local language well.
- Workshops are **always free** for children and teens (0–18). Materials are donated, collected or found in nature.
