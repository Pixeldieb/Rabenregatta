# Tutorials

These tutorials explain **how boats work**. They don't tell you what to build. That part is up to you.

Each tutorial takes 10–20 minutes and includes a small experiment.

| # | Tutorial | You learn |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Why things float](01-why-things-float.md) | Buoyancy, density, how much your boat can carry |
| 2 | [Staying upright](02-staying-upright.md) | Stability, centre of gravity, why boats tip over |
| 3 | [Hull shapes](03-hull-shapes.md) | Pointed or flat? One hull or two? |
| 4 | [Sails and wind](04-sails-and-wind.md) | How wind pushes a boat, and why a keel helps |
| 5 | [Joining without glue](05-joining-without-glue.md) | Lashing, pegging, clamping: strong and creek-friendly |
| 6 | [Build a test harbour](06-build-a-test-harbour.md) | A test tank and a mini creek for home or school |

## Design examples

In [design-examples/](design-examples/) you'll find three **study boats**. Each shows one principle in action.

> ⚠️ **Study, don't copy.** The study boats are for learning only. A copy of a study boat may not race. Take the idea, then build your own boat.

## For teachers

The tutorials fit into science, craft and technology lessons for ages 8–14. Each one follows the same steps:

1. **Wonder:** a question
2. **Try:** a small experiment
3. **Understand:** a short explanation
4. **Use it:** what it means for your boat

This follows the experiential learning cycle ([Kolb, 1984](../wiki/Sources.md#kolb-1984)).
