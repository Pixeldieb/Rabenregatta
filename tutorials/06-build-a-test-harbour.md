# 6. Build a Test Harbour

Engineers test, test and test again. With a test harbour, you can find out if your boat floats, stays upright and goes straight before race day.

## Level 1: The tub

**You need:** a big tub, a storage box, a mortar trough or a paddling pool. At least 40 × 60 cm and 10 cm of water.

**Tests:**

- **Float test:** Does it float? How deep does it sink? Mark the waterline with a pencil.
- **Wobble test:** Push one side down and let go. Does it come back?
- **Soak test:** Leave it in the water for 15 minutes. Is it lower now? (Wood and cardboard soak up water.)
- **Wind test:** Fan air with a piece of cardboard. Does it tip over?

## Level 2: The mini creek

Test how your boat behaves in a current.

**You need:**

- 2–3 metres of rain gutter (from a DIY store or a roofer's leftovers), or two long boards nailed together into a V shape and lined with an old cloth
- Two end caps, or clay to close the ends
- A garden hose
- A bucket at the lower end
- Something to tilt it slightly (a brick under one end)

**Setup:**

1. Put the gutter on a slight slope, on the ground or on two chairs.
2. Leave the lower end open, above the bucket.
3. Let water run in at the top with the hose.

**Tip:** A standard rain gutter is narrower than 21 cm, so your boat may not fit. Then use it to test **small models** of your idea, or use two boards to build a wider channel.

**Tests:**

- **Straight test:** Does the boat go straight or does it turn around?
- **Obstacle test:** Put a few stones in. Does the boat get stuck?
- **Race test:** Race against a friend's boat.

## Level 3: The real creek

If you're allowed to, and **always with an adult**, test at a shallow, safe spot of the creek. Put someone downstream to catch your boat!

## Keep a builder's logbook

| Test no. | What I changed | What happened | What I'll try next |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First version | Tipped over | Make it wider |
| 2 | Added a second hull | Floats, but turns around | Add a fin at the back |
| 3 | ... | ... | ... |

This is the **engineering design process**: build, test, learn, improve. Every row in the table is a small success, even when the boat sank.
