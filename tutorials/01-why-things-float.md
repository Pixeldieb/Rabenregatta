# 1. Why Things Float

## 🤔 Wonder

A huge steel ship floats. A small pebble sinks. Why?

## 🧪 Try

You need: a bowl of water, a pebble, a cork, a piece of wood, a ball of aluminium foil, and the same foil folded into a little boat.

1. Guess first: which ones will float? Write it down.
2. Put each object into the water.
3. What surprised you? The foil ball sinks (or floats badly), but the foil boat floats. Same material, same weight!

## 💡 Understand

When something is in water, it **pushes water aside**. The water pushes back upwards. This push is called **buoyancy**.

The Greek scientist Archimedes discovered the rule more than 2,000 years ago:

> **An object is pushed up with a force equal to the weight of the water it pushes aside.**

- If the object is **lighter** than the water it can push aside, it floats.
- If it is **heavier**, it sinks.

That's why the foil boat floats: its shape pushes a lot of water aside. The foil ball is small, so it only pushes a little water aside.

### Density: light or heavy for its size?

**Density** tells you how heavy something is for its size. Water has a density of about **1 gram per cubic centimetre** (1 g/cm³). Anything with a lower density floats by itself.

| Material | Density (approx., g/cm³) | Floats? |
|---|---|---|
| Cork | 0.12–0.24 | Floats very high |
| Balsa wood | 0.1–0.2 | Floats very high |
| Spruce, pine | 0.4–0.55 | Floats well |
| Oak | 0.6–0.9 | Floats, but low |
| Water | 1.0 | – |
| Plastic bottle (PET, the material) | 1.4 | The material sinks! Air inside makes it float |
| Steel | 7.8 | Sinks, unless shaped like a boat |

*Values vary with type and moisture. Source: typical values from physics and material handbooks, e.g. [OpenStax](../wiki/Sources.md#openstax-physics).*

## 🚤 Use it for your boat

### How much can an A4 boat carry?

An A4 sheet has an area of **21 cm × 29.7 cm ≈ 624 cm²**.

If your boat were a box with an A4 base and sank **1 cm** into the water, it would push aside 624 cm³ of water. That weighs about **624 grams**.

> **Rule of thumb:** For every centimetre an A4 box sinks, it can carry about 600 g.

Real boats are not perfect boxes (the front is pointed, there are gaps), so the real value is lower. But it shows: **even a small boat can carry a lot.**

### Tips

- **Shallow creek?** Your boat should not sink deeper than 2–3 cm, or it will hit stones. Build light, or build wide.
- **Floats you can find:** corks, closed bottles, light wood, hollow reed stems.
- **Wet wood gets heavier.** Test your boat for at least 10 minutes. Beeswax helps keep water out.
