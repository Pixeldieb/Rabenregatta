# 5. Joining Without Glue

Most glues are plastics. They can loosen in water, and many are not great for creeks. Luckily, people built boats for thousands of years with rope, pegs and clever cuts. You can too.

## 🤔 Wonder

How do you hold two corks, three sticks or two bottles together, strongly, without glue?

## 🧪 Try

Take two sticks and try to connect them in a cross shape:

1. With a piece of string, any way you like
2. With a rubber band
3. By cutting a notch into each stick so they slot together

Which one holds best when you twist it?

## 🔗 Techniques

### Lashing (with string)

**Square lashing** connects two sticks at a right angle. It is the classic way scouts build rafts.

1. Tie the string to one stick with a simple knot (clove hitch).
2. Wrap the string around both sticks in a cross pattern, 3–4 times.
3. Then wrap **between** the sticks, around the wrapping, 2–3 times. This is called "frapping" and pulls everything tight.
4. Finish with a knot.

Tip: **wet the string**, especially cotton or jute, before lashing. It shrinks as it dries and gets even tighter. But careful: it also stretches again when wet. Test your boat in water!

### Rubber bands

Quick and easy for holding corks or bottles together. Use 2 per connection in case one breaks. Count your rubber bands at the end. They must not end up in the creek.

### Pegs and dowels

Drill a hole through both parts and push a wooden dowel, skewer or toothpick through. A drop of water makes the wood swell and hold tighter.

For corks: push a skewer lengthwise through a row of corks, like a kebab. Several skewers side by side make a raft.

### Notches and slots

Cut a notch halfway into two pieces of wood and slide them together. It's strong, needs no extra material, and looks great. You need a saw and some patience.

### Clamping

A clothes peg can hold a sail, a mast or a flag. Wooden pegs are on the green list.

## 🍯 Natural "glue" and coatings

| What | Use | Waterproof? |
|---|---|---|
| **Flour paste** (1 part flour, 3 parts water, heat while stirring until thick) | Decoration, paper on wood | No, only above water |
| **Beeswax** (warm it and rub it in) | Makes wood, paper and cloth water-repellent | Water-repellent |
| **Wood glue D3** (yellow list) | Strong wooden joints | Yes, when fully dry (24 h) |
