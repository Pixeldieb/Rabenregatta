# 2. Staying Upright

## 🤔 Wonder

Your boat floats. But then it suddenly flips over. Why?

## 🧪 Try

You need: a piece of wood (about 20 × 5 × 2 cm), a stick as a mast, some modelling clay or a small stone, a bowl of water.

1. Put the wood flat in the water. Push one side down gently and let go. It comes back.
2. Now stick the mast on top and attach the clay **at the top of the mast**. Put it in the water. It flips!
3. Move the clay **under the wood** (below the waterline). Put it in the water. Now it's very stable.

## 💡 Understand

Two points decide whether your boat stays upright:

- **Centre of gravity:** the point where all the weight of the boat seems to pull **down**.
- **Centre of buoyancy:** the point where the water pushes **up**. It is in the middle of the part of the boat that is under water.

When the boat tilts, the underwater part changes shape. The centre of buoyancy moves to the side that goes down, and it pushes that side back up. **As long as the weight is low enough, the boat rights itself.**

If the weight is **too high** (heavy mast, cargo on top), the push from the water can't win. The boat flips.

## 🚤 Use it for your boat

Three ways to make your boat stable:

### 1. Keep weight low

Heavy parts go down, light parts go up. A mast made from a thin stick is better than a thick branch.

### 2. Go wide

A wide boat is harder to tip over than a narrow one. The A4 footprint gives you 21 cm of width. Use it!

A **catamaran** (two hulls side by side) or **trimaran** (three hulls) is very stable, because the floats are far apart.

### 3. Add a keel or ballast

A **keel** is a fin under the boat. Put a little weight at the bottom of it (a stone, a nut or screw that's firmly attached), and it works like the clay in the experiment. Careful: a keel makes your boat deeper. In a shallow creek, keep it short.

## ⚖️ The trade-off

| Choice | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| Tall sail | More wind power | Less stable |
| Deep keel | Very stable, goes straight | Hits stones in shallow water |
| Wide boat | Stable | May get stuck between stones |
| Heavy ballast | Stable | Sinks deeper |

There is no perfect boat. You decide what matters most on **your** creek. That's what engineers do all the time.
