# 4. Sails and Wind

In the Rabenregatta, the creek carries your boat. The wind can help, or push you into the bank.

## 🤔 Wonder

Does a sail always make a boat faster?

## 🧪 Try

You need: two identical simple boats, a stick and a piece of cloth or paper as a sail, a tub of water, a fan or a piece of cardboard to make wind.

1. Put a sail on one boat. Leave the other without.
2. Make wind from behind (the fan behind the boats). Which one is faster?
3. Now make wind **from the side**. What happens to the boat with the sail?

## 💡 Understand

### Wind from behind

A sail catches the wind like an open umbrella. The wind pushes it forward. Simple and effective.

### Wind from the side

The wind pushes the sail sideways, so the boat drifts sideways or tilts. Real sailboats use a **keel** under the water to resist this sideways push. The keel "grips" the water, and the boat moves forward instead of sideways.

### On a creek

On a creek, the wind often comes from changing directions: trees, hedges and bends change it all the time. A big sail can help on a straight, open stretch and cause trouble in bends.

## 🚤 Use it for your boat

| Your creek | Tip |
|---|---|
| Strong current, little wind | Skip the sail. Focus on a good hull and a long underwater part |
| Open, windy, wind usually from upstream | A small square sail can give extra speed |
| Many bends and trees | Small sail or none. Stability matters more |

### Sail tips

- **Material:** cotton cloth, old T-shirts, paper coated with beeswax. Avoid plastic foil. It tears into pieces.
- **Mast:** a skewer, dowel or thin stick. Fix it firmly at the bottom. A wobbly mast breaks.
- **Height:** remember the 42 cm limit, and remember [stability](02-staying-upright.md)! A tall sail needs weight low down.
- **Test** in the test harbour with a piece of cardboard as a "wind machine".
