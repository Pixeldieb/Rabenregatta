# Study Boat: Offcut Keelboat

**Principle:** Going straight with a pointed hull and a keel ([Tutorial 3](../03-hull-shapes.md), [Tutorial 4](../04-sails-and-wind.md))
**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐⭐ Harder (from age 10)

> ⚠️ Study boat, for learning only. Build your own design for the race.

## Materials

| Material | Traffic light |
|---|---|
| Softwood offcut, e.g. spruce, about 28 × 12 × 3 cm | 🟢 |
| Thin wooden strip as keel, about 10 × 4 × 0.5 cm | 🟢 |
| Small stone or large nut/screw as ballast, firmly fixed | 🟢 / 🟡 |
| Wooden dowel or stick as mast, max. 35 cm | 🟢 |
| Cotton cloth scrap as sail | 🟢 |
| Beeswax | 🟢 |

**Tools:** hand saw or jigsaw, cordless drill (power tool station, with an adult next to you), rasp or sandpaper, clamp, safety glasses, gloves

## The idea

A long wooden hull with a pointed front slides easily through the water. A keel underneath keeps it on course and, with a little weight at its bottom end, keeps the boat upright even with a sail.

## How it works

- The **pointed bow** splits the water, so there's less resistance.
- The **keel** resists sideways movement. The boat points downstream instead of turning around.
- The **ballast** at the bottom of the keel lowers the centre of gravity, so the boat can carry a sail without tipping over.

## Building notes

- Draw the outline on the wood first. Clamp the wood before sawing.
- Cut the keel into a slot in the bottom (sawn notch) or fix it with dowels. It must sit firmly.
- **Shallow creek?** Keep the keel short (max. 3–4 cm deep) and round its front edge so it slides over stones.
- Rub the hull with warm beeswax so it absorbs less water.
- Mast: drill a hole, push the dowel in tightly. A drop of water makes it swell and hold.
- Tall masts tip boats over. Boats over about 40 cm (from the bottom of the keel to the top of the mast) usually capsize.

## Testing

- Does it go straight in the mini creek? Compare with and without the keel.
- How much sail can it carry before it tips over?
- Does the keel hit stones? Make it shorter or wider.

## Questions to take further

- Where exactly should the keel be: middle, front, back? Try it.
- Could two short keels work better than one long one?
- What happens if you leave out the sail and make the keel longer?
