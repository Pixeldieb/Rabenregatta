# Study Boat: Carton Catamaran

**Principle:** Stability through width ([Tutorial 2](../02-staying-upright.md))
**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐ Medium (from age 8)

> ⚠️ Study boat, for learning only. Build your own design for the race.

## Materials

| Material | Traffic light |
|---|---|
| 2 empty drink cartons (e.g. 0.5 l), rinsed and closed | 🟡 reused |
| 2–3 sticks or wooden slats as cross beams | 🟢 |
| Cotton string or rubber bands | 🟢 / 🟡 |
| Optional: skewer as mast, cotton cloth as sail | 🟢 |

**Tools:** scissors, awl, ruler

## The idea

Two narrow hulls connected by cross beams. The hulls are far apart, so the boat is very hard to tip over.

## How it works

When the catamaran tilts, one hull is pushed deeper into the water and the other rises. The deeper hull gets a strong upward push, and the boat rights itself. The wider the hulls are apart, the stronger this effect is.

Drink cartons are **waterproof on the inside** (coated with plastic and sometimes aluminium). Closed tightly, they are good floats. That's why they are on the yellow list: reuse them, don't buy them for the boat.

## Building notes

- Keep the cartons **closed** and air-tight. Fold and fasten the opening.
- The whole boat, including the cross beams, must fit inside 21 × 29.7 cm. So the outer edges of the hulls must be at most 21 cm apart.
- Connect beams and hulls with string or rubber bands, not tape (tape comes off in water).
- The connection is the weak point. Test it by gently twisting the boat.

## Testing

- Push one hull down. How strongly does it come back?
- Does the boat go straight, or does it spin?
- What happens to the cartons after 20 minutes in water?

## Questions to take further

- What if the hulls were pointed at the front?
- What else could you use as hulls? Bottles? Wood? Reed bundles?
- Is a trimaran (three hulls) even more stable? Or just heavier?
