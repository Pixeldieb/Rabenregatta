# Study Boat: Cork Raft

**Principle:** Floating with a very light material ([Tutorial 1](../01-why-things-float.md))
**Difficulty:** ⭐ Easy (from age 6)

> ⚠️ Study boat, for learning only. Build your own design for the race.

## Materials

| Material | Traffic light |
|---|---|
| 20–40 natural wine corks | 🟢 |
| 4–6 wooden skewers | 🟢 |
| Cotton string | 🟢 |
| Optional: stick and cloth scrap as a flag or sail | 🟢 |

**Tools:** awl or small hand drill, scissors, safety glasses

## The idea

Cork is **5 to 8 times lighter than water**. A raft of corks floats very high and hardly ever sinks. It's great for a first success.

## How it works

Each cork pushes aside more water than it weighs, so every cork is a small float. Many corks side by side make a wide, flat, stable raft.

## Building notes

- Skewers can go through the corks lengthwise or crosswise. Pre-drill with an awl so the cork doesn't split, and so no one pushes a sharp skewer into their hand.
- Cut or cover skewer tips that stick out. They are sharp.
- String can lash rows of corks together as extra security.

## Testing

- Does the raft stay flat, or does it bend in the middle?
- Does it turn around in the current? What could keep it straight?
- What happens if you put a small stone on it as "cargo"?

## Questions to take further

- A flat raft is stable but slow. How could you give it a pointed front?
- Could you use corks as floats **under** another material?
- How many corks do you really need? Try with half.
